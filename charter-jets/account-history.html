Account History and Real Benchmarks

There are two Google Ads accounts. This document is about 645-044-1013, the one we will build in, and it is the most useful thing we have found so far, because it gives us real numbers from this business instead of industry estimates.

Account: 645-044-1013, "Charter Jets, Inc.", USD, America/New York, auto-tagging on Spend: $67,621 across 10,000 clicks and 50 recorded inquiries Status: everything is paused today Source: Google Ads API, full account history, pulled September 3, 2026

The first thing we found: $12,133 spent on a dead landing page

Every search ad in this account points at lp.charterjetsinc.com. That subdomain is broken:

https://lp.charterjetsinc.com/   ->  SSL certificate has expired
http://lp.charterjetsinc.com/private-jet-charters/   ->  500 Internal Server Error

The DNS still resolves, so the subdomain exists and points at hosting that stopped serving pages. Anyone who clicked an ad got a browser security warning, and then an error page.

The account's own data shows exactly when it happened:

Month Spend Inquiries Avinode search events
September 2025 $2,600 2 281
October 2025 $2,696 2 135
November 2025 $2,486 1 4
December 2025 $2,700 1 7
January 2026 $2,679 0 28
February 2026 $2,354 0 10
March 2026 $1,913 0 4

$12,133 of spend from November onward produced 2 inquiries. Five months of paying for clicks that landed on an error.

This is not a criticism of anyone. It is the single most common way a paid account quietly bleeds: nothing in Google Ads tells you the destination broke. It is also the reason the first item on our launch checklist is a weekly check that the page still loads.

What it changes for us, in a good way

lp.charterjetsinc.com already exists and already belongs to you. It is exactly the subdomain we have been asking for to move the campaign landing page off our domain. We do not need to create anything: we point that subdomain at the new landing page.

That closes the domain question in what we need, and it is why GoDaddy access has moved to the top of that list.

The tracking in this account is sound. We are not rebuilding it.

This is the big difference between the two accounts, and it is worth being explicit because our earlier documents assumed we would have to rebuild measurement from nothing.

Of 33 conversion actions, only five ever fired:

Recorded Action Counted toward bidding In the Conversions column
2,698 Search One Way no no
2,535 Search Round Trip no no
50 Inquiry sent, valued at $200 yes yes
7 Local actions - Website visits yes no
6 Calls from Smart Campaign Ads yes no

The Avinode search events are correctly excluded from bidding and from the Conversions column. That is precisely the error that wasted the other account's budget, and it was not made here. The reported conversion count of 50 matches real inquiries exactly.

Also already correct: geographic targeting is set to Presence on all four campaigns that spent, auto-tagging is on, the inquiry carries a $200 value, and wasted spend on irrelevant search terms is 4.6%, against 44% in the other account. The negative keyword work in this account was done properly.

What still needs cleaning

  • 33 conversion actions, most of them inherited clutter. Dead Universal Analytics goals, two different GA4 properties, and three duplicates of the same inquiry action.
  • The Default / Website goal category is marked as biddable. It does no harm today because the actions inside it are set to secondary. But if anyone ever flips one to primary, bidding silently starts chasing search-widget clicks. We will unset it.
  • Two Google Tag Manager containers are live on the site. We need to confirm they are not double counting.
  • Local actions - Website visits is marked primary. A website visit is not a conversion.

The benchmarks we will plan with

Splitting the healthy period from the broken-landing-page period:

Period Spend Clicks Cost per click Inquiries Click to inquiry Cost per inquiry
Healthy, Mar 2024 to Oct 2025 $52,498 7,247 $7.24 48 0.66% $1,094
Broken, Nov 2025 onward $12,133 1,193 $10.17 2 0.17% $6,066

The real benchmark for this business is a $7.24 cost per click, a 0.66% click to inquiry rate, and $1,094 per inquiry.

Our earlier modelling assumed a 3% to 5% click to lead rate. The measured reality is 0.66%. That is a six-fold difference and it changes the plan, which is worked through in unit economics.

One important caveat. Inquiry sent only counts a request made inside the Avinode search widget. It does not count phone calls, and it does not count any other form. The true lead count was higher than 48. How much higher, nobody knows, and closing that gap is exactly what call tracking is for.

The finding that changes our keyword plan

Grouped by search term theme:

Theme Spend Clicks
Contains "rent" or "rental" $16,626 3,388
Contains "charter company" or "charter service" $582 61

The search term rent a private jet, across its variants, took 1,137 clicks and produced zero inquiries.

We had built a full Rental - Hire ad group on the assumption that rental and charter intent were equivalent. The data says they are not. Rental brings cheaper clicks that do not buy. We are demoting that ad group and moving the budget to the charter company and charter service terms. The revised plan is in keywords.

Desktop converts three times better than mobile

Device Clicks Spend Cost per click Inquiries Rate Cost per inquiry
Desktop 1,955 $18,928 $9.68 22 1.13% $860
Mobile 7,822 $47,203 $6.03 27 0.35% $1,748
Tablet 223 $1,490 $6.68 1 0.45% $1,490

Mobile took 70% of the spend and converted at a third of the rate. Cost per inquiry on desktop is half.

It fits the product: a five figure decision gets made sitting down, not while waiting for a lift. It is also an immediate lever, so we will apply a positive desktop bid adjustment from day one rather than waiting three weeks to discover it.

The phone barely rang

Campaign Call extension impressions Calls
[Florida] 7,478 8
Everything else 80 0

Eight calls from 7,478 call extension impressions. That is 0.1%.

This matters because your game plan is built around the phone call from month 6 onward. On this evidence, the call from the ad was not a meaningful channel here.

There are two possible explanations and we cannot tell them apart without measuring:

  1. This buyer does not call from the ad, they use the website. In which case the call-centric half of the plan needs rethinking.
  2. Calls did happen, but from the website rather than from the ad, and were never tracked. In which case the 0.66% inquiry rate understates real leads, and call tracking matters even more than we said.

Either way the answer is the same: instrument it before spending again.

What we are doing about all of this

  1. We build in this account, not the other one. Sound tracking, clean quality score history on the keywords that matter, and 50 real conversions to calibrate against.
  2. lp.charterjetsinc.com becomes the campaign landing page, pointed at the new page.
  3. Florida first, now confirmed with your own spend rather than our reasoning.
  4. Positive desktop bid adjustment from day one.
  5. Demote the rental ad group, fund charter company and charter service.
  6. Clean up the 33 conversion actions and unset the Default / Website biddable category.
  7. Call tracking before launch, because without it we cannot tell whether 0.66% is the truth or just the part that was measured.
  8. Recalibrate the economics against $1,094 per inquiry. See unit economics.
