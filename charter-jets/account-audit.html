Google Ads Account Audit

Account: 813-172-6321 · USD · America/New_York · auto-tagging on Date: August 31, 2026 · Source: Google Ads API v23, full account history Total historical spend: $4,030.71 across 7 campaigns, 682 keywords and 1,870 search terms

Conversion tracking in this account is not reliable, for the reason set out in finding 1. Every quality judgement below is therefore made on search term relevance, cost per click, click through rate and measurable waste, not on reported conversions.

Summary: where the money went
Historical spend $4,030.71
Clicks 4,036
Average cost per click $1.00
Waste identified by relevance $651 of the $1,481 attributable to terms, or 44%
Average search impression share 10% to 35%

An average cost per click of $1.00 in private jet charter is the loudest signal in this audit. Real cost per click in this vertical in the US runs between $8 and $25 on the keywords that actually produce bookings. Paying a dollar means the ads were winning auctions nobody else wanted: price researchers, people shopping to buy an aircraft, and low cost semi-private seats.

Finding 1: conversion tracking counted clicks inside a search widget

Of the 17 conversion actions in the account, these are marked primary and counted toward bidding:

Conversion action Category Historical conversions
Search One Way Begin checkout 996
Search Round Trip Begin checkout 724
Open inquiry form Begin checkout 242
Change startAirport Default 12 (1,177 in all conversions)
Inquiry sent Request quote 3

These are events from the Avinode web app already embedded on charterjetsinc.com: searching a leg, opening a form, and changing the departure airport. None of them is a lead. Close inquiry form, meaning a visitor closing the form, is also registered as a conversion.

Of roughly 1,974 counted conversions, 3 were real quote requests. That is 0.15%.

This explains the impossible ratios in the history:

Campaign Clicks "Conversions" Reported conversion rate
Search_GA 42 85 202%
Pricing-Search 83 94 113%
Search_CA 485 451 93%
Search_SFL 501 468 93%

All four search campaigns run on Maximise Conversions. For months, automated bidding was buying, with real precision, the person who plays with the search widget and leaves. Google did exactly what it was asked to do.

YouTube follow-on views is also marked primary, in a search-only account.

Finding 2: geographic targeting is open to the world

All 7 campaigns use Presence or interest as their location targeting type.

That means the New York campaign shows ads to anyone on the planet who displays an interest in New York. Someone in Manila searching "private jet new york" enters the auction and can click. It is the most common and most expensive geographic leak in Google Ads, and it is active across the whole account.

The targeting is also inconsistent between campaigns:

Campaign Actual targeting
Search_NYC New York City only. Excludes Teterboro, Westchester, Long Island and the Hamptons, which is where the charter client actually is
Search_SFL 4 Florida metro areas (Miami, Tampa, West Palm, Ft. Myers)
Search_CA All of California
Search_GA All of Georgia

The account restricts where it should open up, and opens to the world where it should restrict.

Finding 4: there are 713 negatives, and all of the above still happened

The account holds 713 unique negative keywords: one shared list of 58, 299 at campaign level (284 of them on the paused Performance Max campaign) and 435 at ad group level.

The problem is not volume, it is criteria. Almost all of them are brand and model names (Gulfstream G650, Phenom 300, Wheels Up, Flexjet, Amalfi Jets). There are almost no intent negatives, and intent is where 27% of the waste comes from.

Two specific issues:

  1. Search_GA does not have the shared list applied at all. It is the campaign with the highest cost per click in the account at $5.36, running without any of the 58 shared negatives.
  2. Single word broad negatives (cheap, affordable, cheapest) sit alongside hyper-specific exact negatives (bajit complimentary flight). This is a list that grew by patching rather than by design.

Finding 5: campaign and ad group structure

Campaign Status Spend Budget Bidding
Pmax Paused $782.43 $65/day Maximise conv
Search_CA Enabled $1,033.59 $75/day Maximise conv
Search_SFL Enabled $922.80 $75/day Maximise conv

Display_Retargeting Paused $77.69 $15/day Manual CPM
Pricing-Search Paused $65.00 $35/day Maximise conv
Search_GA Enabled $225.00 $75/day Maximise conv
  • Search_GA contains an ad group called Pricing_CA with 24 keywords. The California ad group was copied inside the Georgia campaign, so Georgia ads serve keywords written for California.
  • Ad groups have no focus. Charter_NYC holds 396 keywords in a single ad group. With 396 keywords there is no way for one ad to be relevant to any of them.
  • 81 keywords are duplicated across ad groups. private jet rental cost sits in 8 different ad groups, where they compete against each other in the auction and fragment the performance history.
  • Match types: 483 exact, 181 phrase, 18 broad. The proportion is reasonable, but 590 keywords have no quality score data at all, meaning they have never served.
  • Search_GA has Display expansion switched on inside a search campaign.
  • Display_Retargeting spent $77.69 for 99,499 impressions and 57 clicks, a click through rate of 0.06%.

Finding 6: the ads

  • One ad per ad group. With no variant, no test is possible.
  • Half the responsive search ads have 10 headlines instead of 15, giving away combination inventory for free.
  • The same ad runs identically in Florida, New York, California and Georgia. Zero localisation in a product where the departure airport is the selling point.
  • Every ad points at charterjetsinc.com/, the homepage. None points at the matching city page, even though the site already has destination pages (/destination/miami-private-jet-charters/, /destination/teterboro-..., and more).
  • A dynamic keyword insertion headline, {KeyWord:Private Jet Charter}, sits in position 1. With 396 keywords in the ad group, that inserts anything.
  • Policy risk: the headline Over 100 Years Experience, where the description clarifies "combined over a 100 years of experience". On its own the headline claims the company is more than 100 years old. That is misrepresentation, and it is avoidable.

Finding 7: assets and extensions

Present: 8 sitelinks, 4 callouts, a call extension, and images.

Missing:

  • Structured snippets for aircraft categories (Light, Midsize, Super Midsize, Heavy, Ultra Long Range, Turboprop). The most natural asset this business could have, and it does not exist.
  • Price extension and location extension.
  • Sitelinks are generic and shared across all campaigns. None points to /empty-legs/, /safety/ or any destination page.
  • Image assets are named things like Generated image - 2026-08-29 19:28:26_1.199, with several duplicate versions of the same file. Nobody curated them.

84% of spend went to mobile at $0.95 a click. On a high value product that is not a bargain. It confirms that the campaign bought the cheapest and lowest intent inventory it could find, pushed there by the broken conversion goal in finding 1.

We do not recommend touching device adjustments yet. Once keywords, negatives and geography are fixed, the mobile profile will change completely. Measure it then.

Finding 9: impression share

Campaign Impression share Lost to budget Lost to rank CPC
Search_CA 10.0% 88.7% 4.5% $2.13
Search_SFL 17.6% 50.8% 31.6% $1.84
Search_NYC 17.2% 31.8% 50.9% $1.65
Search_GA 35.1% 44.4% 20.5% $5.36

Search_CA loses 88.7% of impression share to budget. The easy reading is "raise the budget". The correct reading is that raising the budget on this structure buys more of the same. Fix what is being bought first, then how much.

Search_NYC loses 50.9% to ad rank, which is a genuine relevance and quality score problem, and a direct consequence of 396 keywords in one ad group served by one generic ad.

Finding 10: the landing page

Every ad points at charterjetsinc.com/. In parallel a new campaign landing page exists, with the Avinode search embedded and lead capture built.

Two notes. The new page currently lives on our domain rather than yours, and should move to a subdomain of charterjetsinc.com before receiving paid traffic. And charterjetsinc.com already has the Avinode web app and GA4 connected, which is where the events in finding 1 originate. The measurement infrastructure exists; it is misconfigured on the Google Ads side, not on the website side.

Verdict

This account does not have an optimisation problem. It has a foundations problem. In order of cost:

  1. It optimised toward a false conversion for its entire history.
  2. Geographic targeting was open to the world in all 7 campaigns.
  3. 44% of spend went to intent that does not buy, and the existing negatives were not built to stop it.

Everything else, oversized ad groups, one ad per group, no structured snippets, no localisation, is correctable in an afternoon. The three above are the reason $4,030 produced 3 real quote requests.

What we do about it is in the roadmap.
