Open Items and Access

Ordered by how much each one blocks. Nothing here is administrative housekeeping. Each item either stops the launch or changes a decision we would otherwise have to guess at.

1. The two remaining business numbers

You have given us the average trip value of $20,000 and the average margin of 10%. Those two unlocked the whole model in unit economics. Thank you, they were the blocker.

Two are still open, and they now matter more than any access on this list:

What we need Why it decides the program
Lead to booked flight close rate Determines whether paid search is profitable at all. The model needs 17.5% to break even at a 10% margin
Average flights per client, lifetime Determines whether a 10% close rate is survivable. At two flights per client the program works comfortably. At one, it does not

Useful but lower priority: flights booked per month today, as a baseline so we can tell what the program added.

One clarification on the margin. Is the 10% gross margin on the full $20,000, meaning $2,000 before your own costs, or is it net of everything? The model assumes the former. If it is net, the picture is better than we have shown. If $2,000 is before staff, software and overhead, it is tighter.

And one question worth asking. A 10% markup on a $20,000 charter is at the low end for a broker. Moving to 15% would drop the close rate needed to break even from 17.5% to 11.7%. Nothing available to us inside Google Ads comes close to that effect. If the 10% is set by competitive pressure rather than policy, it is worth a conversation.

4. Where the leads go, and how fast

Three questions:

  • Who receives an inbound lead, and during what hours?
  • What is the committed response time?
  • Is there a CRM, and if so which one, so leads can be routed into it automatically?

The campaign landing page currently promises that a specialist responds within 15 minutes. That promise needs to be one the sales side can actually keep. In charter, speed of response moves the close rate more than almost anything we can do inside the ad account, and it also determines the ad scheduling we recommend.

5. Call tracking

Your game plan is built around the phone call from month 6 onward, with a target of under $150 per call. Today, no inbound call is attributable to any campaign or keyword, so that target cannot be measured.

The unit economics moved this from important to non-negotiable. If calls close at 20% while form fills close at 5%, then optimising the account toward form fills actively destroys value. We cannot know which is happening without measuring it.

We need a decision on approach, and the account if you already have one. The options and the recommendation are in the measurement plan. One detail worth flagging: the same phone number appears on charterjetsinc.com and on the campaign landing page, so whatever we implement has to cover both.

6. Domain

The campaign landing page currently runs on a subdomain owned by us, not by Charter Jets. Before it receives paid traffic it should move to a subdomain of charterjetsinc.com, for example fly.charterjetsinc.com.

Two reasons. A visitor about to share their phone number for a five figure decision should land on your domain, not an unfamiliar one. And the asset should belong to you.

We need DNS access, or someone on your side who can add a record.
