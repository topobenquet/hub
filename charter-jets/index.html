Charter Jets Inc: Paid Media Program

This is the working hub for the Google Ads program. Every document here is live, meaning it gets updated as the work progresses rather than sent once and forgotten. The short version of where things stand is below, and each document goes deeper.

Where we are today

The account has spent $4,030.71 historically across 7 campaigns and produced 3 real quote requests. That is not a bidding problem or a copy problem. The account was optimising toward a conversion that was not a lead, its geographic targeting was open to the entire world, and 44% of spend went to searches from people who were never going to book a flight.

All three are fixable, and the rebuild is designed and ready to load. What is holding up launch is not campaign work. It is that there is currently no way to measure whether a campaign is succeeding: no GA4 property, no tag manager, no call tracking, and the conversions that do exist in the account count clicks inside a search widget.

We do not recommend spending a dollar of the new budget until that is closed. With $10,000 a month committed, every week of running blind costs money that cannot be recovered.

The three things that matter most right now

1. The economics are tighter than the game plan assumes. With a $20,000 trip at a 10% margin, every booked flight carries $2,000 of gross margin, so $10,000 a month of media has to produce 5 booked flights just to break even. At a realistic 10% close rate it produces about 2.9. The full model, and the four levers that change it, are in unit economics. The short version is that the first flight from a new client is roughly break even and the second is where the money is, which changes what we optimise for.

2. Measurement has to be built before launch, not verified during month 1. Details in the measurement plan.

3. The first month's report will look worse than what you are seeing today. We will go from thousands of recorded "conversions" to a few dozen real leads, and from thousands of cheap clicks to hundreds of expensive ones. That is the goal, not a failure, and we would rather say so in writing now than explain it after the fact.

The two numbers we still need

Everything above is modelled. Two inputs would replace the modelling with your reality:

  • Lead to booked flight close rate. Decides whether the channel is profitable at all.
  • Average flights per client over their lifetime. Decides whether a 10% close rate is survivable. If clients typically fly twice, the program works comfortably. If most fly once, it does not.

Send those two and we will replace every estimate in unit economics with real numbers and set a target we can both hold the campaign to.
