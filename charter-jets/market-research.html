Market and Competitor Research

Research run on September 2, 2026. Two sources, both first party rather than third party estimates: Google's own Keyword Planner through the Google Ads API for demand, and the Google Ads Transparency Center for what competitors are actually running right now.

Part 1: where the demand actually is

We pulled 1,238 keyword ideas across eight themes, US, English. The result reframes the geographic question in a way neither plan anticipated.

The volume is national and generic, not local

private jet rental 12,100 $6.48 High

City qualified terms barely exist

This is the finding that matters most:

Theme Total monthly volume Highest single term
Core charter terms 1,107,640 private jet at 49,500
Price research 615,730 how much is a private jet at 12,100
Purchase intent 57,670 private jet charter cost at 3,600
Empty legs 44,170 empty leg flights at 4,400
Miami specific 6,650 private jet miami at 480
New York specific 6,210 private jet to new york at 390
Routes 3,320 private jet from new york to miami at 260

private jet charter miami is not a meaningful keyword. The entire Miami cluster is 6,650 searches a month nationally, and the single best term inside it is 480. The best route term in the country, New York to Miami, is 260.

The consequence is direct: the Cities ad groups in the campaign plan will be nearly empty, and so will the routes campaign. They are still worth running, because that traffic is extremely high intent and cheap, but they are not where the budget goes.

The real mechanism is generic keywords filtered by campaign geography. Someone in Miami searching private jet charter is the customer. They do not type "Miami" because Google already knows where they are. So the geographic decision is not about which city keywords to buy. It is about who is allowed to see the generic ones.

This also means: at $10,000 a month we are budget constrained, not demand constrained. private jet charter alone is 22,200 searches a month and we can only afford about 714 clicks. Narrowing geography costs us nothing in reachable volume. It just concentrates the same budget into higher impression share in a smaller area.

One more note on the New York cluster

The highest volume terms in the New York set are competitor brand searches:

Term Monthly searches
flexjet teterboro private terminal 880
teterboro private jet charter the early air way 720
private jet to new york 390

That tells us two things. Teterboro is the centre of gravity for New York charter, which confirms the targeting in the campaign structure. And competitors have built enough brand recognition there that people search for them by name, which is a conquesting opportunity and also a warning about what we are up against.

Finding 1: your two closest competitors appear to be one advertiser

The Transparency Center shows the roughly 300 ads pointing at mercuryjets.com as being run by MONARCH AIR GROUP, LLC, while monarchairgroup.com itself carries only 3 ads.

Your game plan treats Monarch and Mercury as two separate benchmarks. On this evidence they are one advertising operation, with the spend concentrated behind the Mercury Jets brand.

Worth confirming independently before acting on it, but if it holds, the competitive picture is simpler than assumed: one dominant direct broker advertiser, not two.

Finding 2: they are winning the long tail airport by airport

Two live Mercury Jets ads, captured verbatim:

Dayton-Wright Brothers Airport Private Jet Charter | Aircraft Hire On Demand Charter. Safe, Reliable and Experienced Provider. Industry Leading Quoting Tool. 1,000+ Excellent Trustpilot Reviews. Expert Team of Aviators.

BYA Private Jet Charter | Boundary Airport On Demand Charter. Safe, Reliable and Experienced Provider. Industry Leading Quoting Tool. 1,000+ Trustpilot 5* Reviews. Expert Team of Aviators. 24/7 Availability.

Dayton-Wright Brothers is a small general aviation field in Ohio. Boundary is smaller still. Neither is a market anyone would target deliberately as a city.

That is the strategy: roughly 300 near identical ads, one per airport, each targeting a handful of searches a month with almost no competition, at a very low cost per click. It matches exactly what the Keyword Planner showed, that individual geographic terms have tiny volume. They are not fighting for the head terms. They are carpeting the tail.

This is a real strategic option for Charter Jets that appears in neither plan. The United States has more than 5,000 public airports. The site already has destination pages. The approach is cheap, defensible, and it compounds. It is not a month 1 move, because it needs page infrastructure, but it belongs on the roadmap.

Finding 3: their message, and the one thing we cannot match yet

Every Mercury ad repeats the same block:

Their claim Our position
On Demand Charter Same. We can match this
Safe, Reliable and Experienced Provider Same. Part 135, ARG/US, Wyvern
Industry Leading Quoting Tool We are stronger here and not saying so
1,000+ Trustpilot 5* Reviews We have nothing
Expert Team of Aviators Same
24/7 Availability Same

Two observations.

They claim a quoting tool. We have a better one and do not mention it. Their tool is a quote request form. The landing page carries live Avinode availability and estimated pricing across more than 6,000 aircraft. That is a genuinely stronger product claim, and right now neither our ads nor our landing page make it.

The review count is their real moat. "1,000+ Trustpilot 5* Reviews" appears in every ad. In a vertical full of brokers with no physical presence, that is the single most persuasive thing in the auction, and we cannot answer it. This is the same trust gap identified from the landing page side in landing and conversion, confirmed from the competitor side.

Building a review base is a business project, not a campaign task, and it should start now, because it takes months and it will keep costing us auctions until it exists.

Finding 4: the operators are not our competition

NetJets, VistaJet and Flexjet each run 200 to 300 ads, and VistaJet runs video. Their budgets are ten to fifty times ours.

They are also selling a different product: fractional ownership and jet cards, a multi-year commitment. They are useful to study for tone and for landing page craft, as your plan correctly says. They are not who we lose auctions to.

The one place they matter is negatives. Their brand terms and product terms are already blocked in the negative keyword lists.

Part 3: what this means for the plan

On geography

Given that volume is national and filtered by geography, and that we are budget constrained rather than demand constrained:

  • Adding markets does not add reachable volume. It divides the same 714 clicks.
  • 4 states gives roughly 6 clicks per state per day. 2 states gives 12. 1 state gives 24.
  • Every market beyond Miami needs its own landing page or it damages Quality Score.

We recommend launching in two markets, Florida and New York. The reasoning is in the roadmap, and it is a change from our earlier recommendation of Florida alone. Two things moved it: Florida is where the dominant direct competitor is based, so a second market gives us a comparison that is not distorted by their strength, and Florida and New York are two ends of the same corridor rather than two unrelated markets.

On what we buy

The Keyword Planner numbers put a hard ceiling on the price research campaign. private jet cost and how much is a private jet carry enormous volume, 9,900 and 12,100 a month, at low bids of around $3.94. That looks tempting and it is the trap the account already fell into: 26.8% of historical spend went there. Against a $350 target cost per lead and a required 17.5% close rate, curiosity traffic cannot pay. It stays capped and on a short leash.

On the long tail

The airport level approach Mercury is running is the most interesting thing this research surfaced. It is cheap, it compounds, and it fits a broker with a small budget far better than competing on head terms. It requires landing page infrastructure first, so it is a month 3 or 4 project, but it should be planned for rather than discovered later.
