Launch and Scale Roadmap

Budget: USD 10,000 per month, or $328.77 per day Starting markets: Florida and New York (changed from four, see below) Match type at launch: exact match only Break even requirement: 5 booked flights per month, see unit economics

The arithmetic, before anything else

On exact match keywords that genuinely produce bookings, cost per click in this vertical in the US runs between $8 and $25, not the $1.00 the account history shows. That $1.00 is the price of buying bad traffic.

At $10,000 a month and a realistic $14 cost per click:
Clicks per month around 700
Clicks per day around 23
Split across 4 states around 6 clicks per state per day
At a 3% inquiry rate around 21 requests per month
At 5% around 35 requests per month

Three consequences follow, and all three are worth agreeing on now.

First, going from 4,036 historical clicks to around 700 a month will look like a collapse in any report that counts clicks. It is exactly what we are trying to do: stop buying 3,300 clicks from people who were never going to book.

Second, that conversion volume is low for Google's automated bidding to learn from. Which is why bidding starts manual, and why the offline conversion import in month 2 matters so much.

Third, and most important, those 714 clicks have to produce 5 booked flights a month for the media to pay for itself at a 10% margin. The full model is in unit economics. It is the number the whole program is judged against.

The geographic decision: two markets, not four

We originally planned four states, and then recommended concentrating on Florida alone. After the market research, the recommendation is two: Florida and New York.

The research changed the reasoning. City qualified keywords barely exist in this vertical: the entire Miami cluster is 6,650 searches a month and the best route term in the country is 260. Volume comes from generic keywords filtered by campaign geography. So the geographic question is not which city keywords to buy, it is who is allowed to see the generic ones.

That means at $10,000 a month we are budget constrained, not demand constrained. private jet charter alone is 22,200 searches a month and we can afford 714 clicks. Narrowing geography costs nothing in reachable volume. It concentrates the same budget into higher impression share in a smaller area.

Markets Clicks per market per day Verdict
4 states about 6 Too thin to learn anything anywhere
2 states about 12 Workable, and gives a comparison
1 state about 24 Cleanest, but no comparison

Why two rather than one. Florida is where the dominant direct competitor is based, so Florida alone would tell us how we perform in our opponent's strongest market and nothing else. And Florida and New York are not two unrelated markets: they are the two ends of the same corridor, which the routes campaign serves from both directions.

The condition: New York needs its own landing page before launch. Sending someone who searched from Teterboro to a page headlined Private Jet Rentals in Miami damages ad relevance and landing page experience, which raises cost per click at exactly the moment we cannot afford it. If the New York page is not ready, we launch Florida only and add New York when it is.

We are not repairing the old campaigns. Their learning history is contaminated by months of false conversions, they contain ad groups of up to 396 keywords, and their quality scores reflect it. Building alongside costs less and performs better.

Five campaigns and 12 ad groups, down from seven and twenty. Florida takes the larger share because it is the densest charter market in the US and where the company is already positioned. New York carries the highest value per trip.

Texas and California are not cancelled, they are sequenced. They open in month 5 with their own landing pages, on a structure that has already been proven in two markets.

Phase 2, weeks 2 to 4: clean and calibrate

  • Search term review every 48 hours for the first two weeks, then weekly. Even on exact match, close variants get through, and that is where the negative list keeps growing.
  • Add every non-buying intent found to the shared negative lists.
  • When an ad group accumulates 10 or more clicks with no signal at all, lower its bid rather than pausing it.
  • Only at 30 or more real conversions per month do the higher volume campaigns move to automated bidding with a target cost per acquisition. Not before.
  • Begin uploading offline conversions using the click identifier the landing page already stores with every lead. This is the piece that separates an account that scales from one that plateaus.

Phase 3, month 2: widen the funnel under control

Once exact match has produced a reference cost per acquisition:

  1. Open phrase match in the ad groups that are already working, in separate campaigns with their own budgets, never mixed into the same ad group as exact.
  2. Switch on the empty legs campaign. The Avinode search on the landing page already has an empty legs tab, and the account history shows real searches for it. It is inexpensive transactional traffic that is currently on the negative list only because there was nowhere to send it. Now there is.
  3. Expand the route set with whatever appears in the search term reports.

Phase 4, month 3: scale

  • Raise budgets only in the campaigns whose qualified lead cost is below target.
  • Mirror the validated structure into Microsoft Ads.
  • Open Texas and California, with their own landing pages, on the proven template.
  • Evaluate Performance Max only at 50 or more real conversions per month and with offline conversion import running. The reasoning is in the game plan review.
  • Evaluate Demand Gen for retargeting people who used the search but did not request a quote. That audience exists today and is going to waste.
  • Open the fifth and sixth markets using the validated template.

The risks worth stating plainly

  1. The month 1 report will look worse than today's on clicks and on conversions. We will go from roughly 2,000 phantom conversions to roughly 20 real leads. Agreed in advance, this is progress. Discovered afterward, it looks like failure.
  2. Even two markets may prove thin. $10,000 across Florida and New York gives about 12 clicks per market per day. If the data is still too diluted after three weeks, the recommendation will be to concentrate everything on Florida until there is signal.
  3. Without call tracking, a large share of the return will be invisible. In charter, the high value buyer picks up the phone. With a required 17.5% close rate, this stops being a reporting nicety: if calls close at 20% and forms close at 5%, optimising toward forms actively destroys value, and today we cannot tell the difference.

  4. The real bottleneck may not be Google Ads. If a lead waits four hours for a response, no cost per acquisition will save it. The response time commitment needs to be agreed before launch. At these economics the close rate is a P&L input, not an operational detail: the gap between a 10% and a 20% close rate is the gap between losing $4,300 a month and making $1,400.

  5. On the numbers you have given us, the first flight from a new client is roughly break even at best. The program becomes clearly profitable on the second. That is a normal shape for a high value considered purchase, but it means repeat business is part of the acquisition maths rather than a bonus, and it moves referral and repeat work much earlier than month 9.
