Landing Page and Conversion

The campaign landing page is a dedicated page built for paid search, separate from charterjetsinc.com. It carries the live Avinode search, first party lead capture with full campaign attribution, and the content needed to earn a good landing page experience score from Google.

This document covers what it is, what we measured on it, and what we are changing.

Why a dedicated page rather than the main site

Quality Score has three components, each graded against every other advertiser who showed an ad for the same search over the last 90 days:

Component Driven by Does the landing page move it?
Expected click through rate Ad copy, extensions, keyword history No
Ad relevance How closely the ad text matches the keyword Not directly, but the page reinforces it
Landing page experience Relevance, usefulness, navigability, transparency, speed, mobile Yes

A campaign page lets us match the message to the keyword exactly, put the live search above the fold, and capture the click identifier against every lead. The main site cannot do the last of those, and it is what makes offline conversion import possible later.

What the page does today

  • Live aircraft and pricing search, powered by Avinode, above the fold
  • Lead capture that stores the Google click identifier and campaign parameters with every submission, then redirects to a dedicated thank you page used as the conversion URL
  • Six aircraft categories with real exterior photography, from turboprop to ultra long range
  • Popular routes out of Miami
  • Pricing transparency section explaining what actually drives a charter price
  • Miami departure airports, covering Opa-Locka, Miami International, Miami Executive and Fort Lauderdale Executive
  • Eight FAQs with structured data, covering cost, lead time, airports, pets, luggage, empty legs, the broker model and what is included
  • Broker disclosure and estimated pricing language throughout

What we measured

Measured in production on September 2, on a 375 by 812 mobile viewport.

Two things previously recorded as risks turned out to be fine, and are now closed:

Previously flagged Actual
The search widget might display prices in euros to a US audience It displays USD. Closed
The widget takes 8 to 12 seconds to render It renders in 3.8 seconds

The second measurement led to a more useful finding:

load event ......... 2,730 ms
bootstrap starts ... 2,749 ms   <- the widget only begins here
iframe rendered .... 3,828 ms

The connection to Avinode was already pre-warmed, so network latency was not the constraint. The page was deliberately holding the widget back until after everything else finished loading, in order to protect a page speed score. For a paid traffic page where the search is the conversion, that is the wrong trade. We have changed it to start as soon as the page structure is ready.

Changes made

# Change Reason
1 Search widget starts loading immediately rather than after everything else Recovers roughly 2.7 seconds of empty card at the exact point of conversion
2 The search card now has a heading It was an unlabelled white box. A first time visitor from an ad had to work out what it was
3 All 10 aircraft and route cards now scroll to the search, not to the form at the bottom Someone clicking "Heavy Jet" or "Miami to Aspen" has declared their intent. Sending them to a blank six field form threw that away and asked them to retype it
4 Trust signals moved above the search card On mobile they previously sat below the fold, so the safety credentials were invisible at the moment of decision
5 A call to action for the phone sits directly under the search The call is the highest value lead and it was represented only by a small button in the corner
6 Six real exterior aircraft photographs, one per category, compressed to between 23 and 81 KB The previous set mixed interiors and exteriors and included a category that was not an aircraft type
7 Testimonials replaced with real client messages The page previously carried invented testimonials with fictional names and job titles. That is a genuine compliance exposure on a page receiving paid traffic
8 Shorter subheadline Frees vertical space above the fold on mobile
9 New "Why this is different" section Your closest competitor claims an "industry leading quoting tool" in every ad. Ours is genuinely stronger and the page never said so
10 New About section, with accreditations The page carried no company story and no credentials at all
11 Every internal link now scrolls to the search instead of opening a separate page Keeps the visitor on one page and on one conversion path
12 Search button added to the closing section, alongside the callback form Gives the two conversion paths equal weight at the bottom of the page

What still needs a decision

These are outside our control and each one costs conversions until it is closed.

1. The Avinode commission is set to 0%

At 0%, the prices a visitor sees in the search are wholesale operator pricing. When a specialist follows up with a real quote including your markup, the number will be higher than what the visitor was shown.

That happens at the worst possible moment in the funnel, when the lead is warm and trust is everything. This is a configuration setting, not a page problem, and it is the largest revenue risk currently on the page.

2. The domain

The page runs on a subdomain we own rather than one you own. A buyer about to share their phone number for a five figure decision lands on a domain that does not match the logo at the top of the page. That is a conversion problem as much as an operational one, and it also costs Quality Score.

3. The departure field starts empty

The search widget opens with an empty "From" field, on a page titled Private Jet Rentals in Miami, receiving traffic from ads targeted at Miami. Pre-filling Miami removes a step. This requires a change on Avinode's side.

4. Empty legs is a top level tab in the search

Empty leg hunters generate a high volume of clicks and close at the lowest rate of any segment. Right now the paid landing page invites them in through the front door. Our recommendation is to remove that tab from this page and give empty legs its own campaign and its own page, which is planned for month 2 of the roadmap.

5. Trust signals: partly closed, one gap left

Now on the page: the About section with the company story, ARG/US Registered Broker (rated since 2022) and NBAA membership.

The ARG/US Registered Broker rating is the strongest credential you have and it was nowhere on the site. It matters because it is yours, not the operators'. ARG/US and Wyvern safety ratings belong to the carriers; Registered Broker is Charter Jets Inc.

Two logos we deliberately left off, and why. The FAA seal and the IS-BAO mark both appear on the corporate site. On a paid landing page they are a policy risk. Charter Jets is a broker: it is not certificated by the FAA, and IS-BAO is a standard for flight operations departments, not brokers. Displaying either in an accreditations strip implies a credential the company does not hold, which is exactly the kind of thing Google's misrepresentation policy targets, in an account that already carries one misrepresentation flag from the Over 100 Years Experience headline. The accurate version is on the page as text: every flight is operated by an FAA Part 135 certified air carrier, selected using ARG/US and Wyvern safety ratings.

The gap that remains is reviews. Your closest competitor puts "1,000+ Trustpilot 5 star reviews" in every single ad, and that is the most persuasive thing in the auction. Charter Jets has a Trustpilot profile but the review count is currently too low to display, so we have deliberately left it off the page rather than show a weak number.

That makes building the review base a business project that should start now, not a campaign task. It takes months, and until it exists we keep losing auctions on it. The cheapest version is asking every completed trip for a review as part of the existing post-flight follow up.

6. The form at the bottom asks for six required fields

Origin, destination, date, name, phone and passenger count. For someone who has already used the search at the top, it asks for everything again. For someone who simply wants a call back, it is three times more than necessary. We recommend cutting it to name, phone and optional email.

A note on testing

At roughly 700 clicks and 20 to 35 leads a month, there is not enough traffic to run valid A/B tests. A properly powered two-armed test needs hundreds of conversions per arm, which at this volume would take more than a year to accumulate.

That has a practical consequence worth being explicit about: what is clearly broken gets fixed, not tested. The changes listed above were made on that basis. Formal testing enters the picture if and when spend increases substantially.

In the meantime, Microsoft Clarity gives session recordings and heat maps at any traffic level, for free, and answers the questions statistics cannot answer here. It is on the measurement checklist.

The FAQ is the strongest part of the page

Worth saying, because it is unusual. The eight questions cover the real objections, they are specific rather than generic, and they carry structured data so they are eligible to appear directly in search results.

The one objection the page does not yet answer is what happens if a client needs to change or cancel a flight. That is in what we need, because only you can answer it.
